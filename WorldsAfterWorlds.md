# Worlds After Worlds
## Prologue
The thought started when I had the urge to make a modpack curated just for my group of friends. I figured it'd be a good worldbuilding practice if I could make the gameplay and/or the world generation lore-related. In my mind it was a flawless plan, write the background, choose fitting mods and let everyone have fun. I was essentially designing an experience, a whole game for them.

But I didn't want to just follow Minecraft's easy path of "medieval" and "fantasy" themes. I thought it'd be a bit too cliche, so I first started attempting a post-apocalyptic world. And since I'm ever so obsessed with religion and gods and higher forms of life, I couldn't resist myself from beginning there. 

Essentially the first idea was that the 'apocalypse' was or was caused by the fall of these primordial gods. It was a no-brainer to make them the manifestations of the biomes/dimensions themselves like the land, the sea, the nether and the end. But no matter how much time I really spent on it, it never really satisfied me where the story was leading.

I tried a lot of variations, but it was clear that even if I knew exactly what I wanted. Even if I knew what happened before the world, as the players would know it came to be, I didn't know what the world itself would be now.

Again and again I rewrote and remade the gods, the way they fell and what caused it all but I couldn't answer one question that stayed in the back of my head:
*What happens at the end of everything?*

## Cosmic Inspirations
The main sources that I drew my interpretation of the primordials was real life cosmology and the lore of the primordial monsters in adventure time. It has always intrigued me the very idea of countless monsters existing before us, before everything and before nothing. The visual image of a literal *sea of monsters* that filled every inch of a boundless void scratched a very specific spot in my brain that I just couldn't ignore. And since my mind was running along the lines of the "aftermath" of a universal event birthing something that we would call home, it lead me straight to the big bang, and the formation of our celestial bodies from the nebulae that spread across a space that knew no time. 

Credits to Curious Archive and their absolutely brilliant video on [The Beauty of Games With Dead Worlds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58lztpgwxuk). It introduced to me the idea of "whalefall" and how a god-like being could still harbor life even after it drew it's final breaths. Whalefall is when a whale reaches the end of it's lifespan and the resulting carcass settles on the ocean floor. It's sheer size and amount of potential nutrients attract all forms of life, essentially creating a mini-ecosystem shouldered by one single death. The localized ecological home could thrive for 50 to 100 years, potentially outliving other whales that would've been alive when it all started. Isn't it beautiful? For something to have such an effect after it has lost all will and ability.
